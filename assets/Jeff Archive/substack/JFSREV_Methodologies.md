# JFSREV Methodologies / Frameworks

## Scope and method

This is a reconstruction of Jeff Sun, CFTe (@jfsrev)'s observable approach from the free-text corpus supplied on disk: 19 free posts in *My Market Diary*, dated September 2022–April 2023. It is not a reconstruction from his paid archive, private material, Twitter threads embedded as previews, or later guides. Where the posts show a recurring practice, it is described as a method; where the evidence is incomplete, the text says so.

## 1. Who he is / thesis of his method

The scrape report identifies Jeff Sun, CFTe, publisher of *My Market Diary*, whose publication description is “My Market Diary & Intermittent Reflection On Life As A Trader.” The free-post footers describe him variously as a full-time trader, accredited investor, founder of AM firms in Singapore, and a participant in the 2021 United States Investing Championship (reported return: +89.8%, eighth place). Those are biographical claims reproduced by the corpus, not independently verified performance evidence.

The clearest thesis visible in the posts is a **top-down, technically expressed momentum method**. He begins with the market regime—macro catalysts, rates, the dollar, credit, volatility and breadth—then studies index trend and relative strength, and finally curates individual momentum candidates. Price action, moving averages, classical levels, anchored VWAP, market breadth, relative strength, and price/volume contraction recur. The process is conditional rather than declarative: for an index he normally states a support level, a bull case, and a bear case, with the moving average or prior low that would confirm or invalidate the view.

This is not presented as a fundamental valuation system or a fixed “buy-and-hold” allocation model. Earnings, central-bank decisions and economic releases matter mainly as catalysts that can change the tape, rates and risk appetite. The practical edge appears to be **selectivity plus risk control**: follow strong names, wait for a properly formed setup, avoid chasing an overextended move, and reduce exposure when the broad tape deteriorates.

## 2. Core philosophy and temperament

Three principles are explicit or strongly recurrent.

1. **Wrong mindset is nearly fatal.** In “The Right Mindset In The Market,” Sun writes that the right attitude and mental discipline do not guarantee profitability, but the wrong mindset “almost guarantees certain failure.” The post is brief and links to a Twitter thread, so the free Substack text does not supply a full psychological checklist. Still, it establishes discipline as a necessary condition rather than a promise of returns.
2. **Trading is a long apprenticeship, not a shortcut to income.** “Hard Truth, Realities, And Why ‘Trading For A Living’ Is The Ultimate Grail” flags a distinction between trading for a living and merely being a full-time trader, but its detailed explanation is outside the visible free text. The safe conclusion is only that he treats the distinction as important; the corpus does not justify filling in his missing argument.
3. **Success includes life balance.** In the April 2023 reflection, Sun rejects a purely balance-sheet definition of success. He records a veteran broker’s emphasis on mindset, happiness and balance, and concludes that a trader who performs well while leading a balanced life represents trading success. This is consistent with a temperament that values durability and contentment over a single winning period or headline return.

The weekly posts also show a willingness to revise. A rally can be called a tradable rally while the medium-term downtrend remains intact; a lower volatility reading can weaken a bearish signal; a CPI rally can be recognized as powerful but still treated as a relief rally if the yield curve remains inverted. The method therefore separates **short-term opportunity** from **higher-time-frame regime** rather than forcing one all-or-nothing forecast.

## 3. Markets and instruments

The recurring dashboard is deliberately multi-asset and cross-sectional:

- **Equity benchmarks:** S&P 500 ($SPX/$SPY), Nasdaq 100 ($QQQ), equal-weight comparators ($RSP, $QQQE), and occasionally the Dow and Russell 2000.
- **Crypto:** Bitcoin/USD ($BTCUSD), analyzed with the same moving-average, support/resistance, pattern and volume language as equities.
- **Rates and credit:** 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields (including $TNX), the yield curve, and an $IEI/$HYG credit-spread proxy.
- **Volatility and options sentiment:** equity put/call ratio ($PCCE), VIX, and the VIX/VXV one-month-to-three-month volatility ratio.
- **Breadth and participation:** percentages of stocks above 20-, 50- and 200-day averages; net highs/lows; sector leadership; heat maps; and the NAAIM Exposure Index.
- **Stocks:** weekly “trading ideas,” momentum leaders, sector groups, and focus/stalk lists. The examples range across technology, energy, materials, consumer, China-linked names and smaller growth companies.

The important point is not that every indicator is a trade trigger. Sun uses them as a **regime and confirmation stack**. For example, a widening credit spread is described as a leading warning of difficult economic conditions; higher Treasury yields pressure high-valuation growth; VIX above 30 indicates large volatility; and VIX/VXV above one signals uncertainty. These inputs influence how aggressively he wants to participate in individual breakouts.

## 4. Setup selection and entry criteria

The free posts do not publish a complete mechanical entry checklist. They do, however, reveal a repeatable selection logic:

1. **Start with market posture.** Read $SPX and $QQQ against key moving averages, trendlines, anchored VWAP and recent support. A candidate is more attractive when the relevant index and breadth support risk-taking, and less attractive when the index is under declining averages or credit/rates are worsening.
2. **Prefer leadership and relative strength.** Sun repeatedly labels his focus as “Momentum Leaders,” sometimes adding “RS” (relative strength). In the December diary he says he was adding China names to his stalk list because of their relative strength and momentum. Relative strength is comparative: $QQQ versus $SPX, $QQQ versus $QQQE, or a stock/sector versus the broad tape.
3. **Wait for contraction and a catalyst/level.** His recurring footer and market notes refer to “Price & Volume Contraction.” The Bitcoin analyses describe contraction as a condition from which a bearish pennant or flag may resolve. In stock selection, the implied preference is for a name coiling in a constructive range, not one already consumed by a vertical move.
4. **Demand a breakout that is not too extended.** The most concrete entry evidence is the December 3, 2022 diary. He passed on several breakouts in his focus/stalk list because they were “not ripened”: their day range was greater than ATR and the resulting risk/reward ratio was unattractive. This is a clear anti-chase rule and an explicit link between volatility, entry distance and expected reward.
5. **Define the decision level.** Weekly index notes state the support to watch, then a bull case and bear case. Translated to a stock setup, the same logic suggests entry only when price confirms the intended direction and a nearby support/invalidation level allows acceptable RRR. That last sentence is an inference from his documented practice, not a published formula.

A useful reconstruction is therefore: **regime → leadership → contraction/base → confirmation/breakout → ATR-aware RRR check**. The corpus does not state a minimum R multiple, exact moving-average crossover, volume threshold, or order type.

## 5. Risk, stops and sizing

Risk management is central in posture, but the free text does not provide a stop-distance formula or a fixed percentage risk per trade. The scrape report records that his profile bio says “16 years of stop losses, pain & grit to $10M multi-asset,” but this is profile metadata, not a free-post explanation of how stops are placed. It should not be expanded into rules that are absent from the corpus.

What can be evidenced is:

- He uses **support and moving-average breaches** as explicit risk landmarks. For example, a break of a rising 10/20-day average is repeatedly used as the bear case for $SPX or $QQQ; a break of a prior low is the next downside trigger.
- He treats **ATR and RRR before entry** as risk controls. A breakout already travelling beyond the day’s ATR can be rejected because the remaining reward does not compensate for the chase.
- He **changes aggregate exposure**. On December 7 he described “another day of shaving down exposure” during a broad orderly selloff. This is portfolio-level risk reduction, not merely a stop on one ticker.
- He monitors **correlated risk** through indices, rates, credit, volatility and breadth. A weak tape can make several apparently different stock setups one common equity bet.

Thus the strongest evidence supports a “protect capital and preserve optionality” posture. Exact position sizing, stop placement after entry, gap handling, leverage, maximum portfolio heat and loss limits remain unknown from the free posts.

## 6. Trade management and exits

There is no complete exit playbook in the free corpus. The diary does show active management rather than passive holding: Sun refers to unrealized trades, sitting out a day, shaving exposure, and revising support levels as the tape changes. He also distinguishes a tradable short-term rally from a sustainable trend. This suggests management through evolving technical levels and market context.

Observable exit or de-risking signals include:

- a breach of rising 10/20-day averages or a defined support level;
- rejection at a declining 50/200-day average, downtrend line or classical resistance;
- deteriorating breadth, relative-strength divergence, or an adverse VIX pivot;
- a breakout becoming too extended for the remaining RRR;
- broad market deterioration that warrants reducing exposure even before every individual chart fails.

The posts do not establish whether he takes partial profits, trails stops, uses fixed targets, holds through earnings, or lets winners run by a specified rule. Those details should not be invented.

## 7. Multi-asset and portfolio approach

Sun’s analysis is multi-asset in **monitoring and risk context**: stocks are read alongside Bitcoin, Treasury yields, credit spreads, the dollar, options sentiment and manager exposure. The dashboard helps distinguish a healthy broad advance from a narrow or macro-fragile rally. For example, in November he called the sharp post-CPI equity advance a relief rally whose sustainability was undermined by yield-curve inversion.

The corpus does not disclose a strategic asset-allocation policy, target weights, a hedge ratio, or a formal rotation model. “Multi-asset” should therefore mean a cross-market decision process, not a documented portfolio recipe.

## 8. Process, journaling and review habits

The publication itself is a market diary, and the posts show a weekly review rhythm: market percentage changes, the economic calendar, sector leaders/laggards, breadth, technicals, volatility, rates/credit, NAAIM exposure and trading ideas. The shorter diary entries add day-level observation of unrealized positions, focus/stalk lists and exposure changes.

The apparent workflow is:

- **Before the week:** identify macro events and possible tape-moving catalysts.
- **At the index level:** map trend, moving averages, support, resistance and bull/bear scenarios.
- **Across the market:** inspect breadth, sectors, relative strength, volatility, credit and rates.
- **At the stock level:** maintain a focus/stalk list and select momentum leaders with acceptable structure and RRR.
- **After the session/week:** record what happened, whether a setup was ripe, and whether exposure should be increased or reduced.

The free text does not show a personal trade journal template, win/loss attribution, statistics, screenshots of executions or a formal review scorecard. “Diary” is evidenced; a detailed journaling system is not.

## 9. Frameworks, named systems and the 17-chapter guide

The recurring frameworks visible in free posts are best described as working lenses rather than officially named systems:

- **Weekly Market Picture:** macro calendar plus sectors, breadth, index technicals, volatility, credit/rates and ideas.
- **Momentum Leaders / Relative Strength:** search for stocks and groups outperforming the relevant benchmark.
- **Price and Volume Contraction:** look for compression before resolution; treat contraction patterns in context rather than as certainty.
- **Bull Case / Bear Case:** predefine the reclaim level and breakdown level for an instrument.
- **ATR-to-RRR filter:** decline an otherwise appealing breakout when the move is too extended relative to its risk/reward.
- **Exposure management:** increase or reduce aggregate participation as the breadth and index picture changes.

No 17-chapter outline appears in the free post bodies supplied here. The scrape report says the profile bio advertises “My 17-Chapter Trader’s Guide in Post,” but the five paywalled archive entries—including later guide material—were not collected. The available free text also contains only brief teasers for some linked Twitter threads. Consequently, there is no evidence-backed way to list the 17 chapter titles or claim that the outline is present in this corpus.

## 10. What he avoids / anti-patterns

The clearest avoidances are:

- **Chasing extended breakouts:** a move beyond ATR can make the RRR unattractive.
- **Treating one data print as a regime change:** a strong CPI rally can still be a relief rally when the yield curve is inverted.
- **Ignoring higher-time-frame resistance:** reclaiming a short moving average does not automatically reverse a medium-term downtrend or clear the 200-day average.
- **Trading without breadth/participation:** index strength is read alongside net highs/lows and stocks above moving averages.
- **Staying fully exposed through broad deterioration:** the diary explicitly records shaving exposure.
- **Equating full-time status with sustainable livelihood:** the distinction is raised as a “hard truth,” though the detailed rationale is not free text.
- **Defining success only by money:** the April reflection emphasizes happiness, contentment and life balance.

## 11. Playbook cheat-sheet

- Establish the weekly macro/rates/credit backdrop.
- Check $SPX/$SPY and $QQQ for trend, moving-average posture, VWAP/trendline and key levels.
- Check breadth, sector leadership and net highs/lows; do not assume an index move is broad.
- Compare leadership and relative strength across sectors, benchmarks and candidate stocks.
- Favor momentum leaders with constructive price/volume contraction or a clearly defined range resolution.
- Before buying a breakout, compare the entry with ATR, nearby support and realistic reward; pass if the move is already extended.
- Write the bull case, bear case, support and invalidation level before acting.
- Use VIX/PCCE, VIX/VXV, Treasury yields and credit spreads to calibrate exposure.
- Reduce aggregate exposure when breadth, index averages or market internals deteriorate.
- Review the week in diary form; separate a tradable bounce from a durable trend reversal.
- Treat mindset, durability and life balance as part of success—not substitutes for risk control.

## 12. Caveats

This document covers only the free corpus dated September 2022 through April 2023. The scrape report identifies 19 free posts and five paywalled archive entries; paid access was not attempted. Several free posts are market-picture summaries, while the mindset and “trading for a living” posts point to Twitter threads whose detailed text is not present in the saved Substack body. The 17-chapter guide structure is therefore unavailable here. The reconstruction is descriptive, includes explicit inference where necessary, and is not investment advice or a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security.
